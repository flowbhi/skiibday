Getting a bank's messaging platform back to the standard

Societe Generale · Business Analyst · DFP message processing platform
TL;DR: Downstream applications had piled team-specific rules into our bank-wide messaging platform over the years, which made market updates and client onboarding harder. My analysis showed that the planned fix would only move the problem somewhere else. I proposed a standards-first design instead, got the downstream team to agree to it, and set up the testing strategy for the build.
Read the full case study

Context

DFP is the message-processing platform my team runs for an entire business line. It handles a wide range of formats, including ISO 20022 messages (such as pacs and reda), SWIFT MT, JSON, CSV, Excel and flat text files, carrying transaction notifications and internal files between the bank's applications, external clients and internal teams. For SWIFT traffic, it uses Prowide's converter API to translate between MT and ISO 20022 (MX) formats in both directions.

The problem

Over time, downstream applications asked for their own variations of the standard. For example, the penalties application (PIM) needed certain fields filled with its own values instead of the standard ones, so DFP added hardcoded, team-specific logic to reshape messages before delivering them.

This worked, but it didn't scale:

  • Yearly SWIFT releases got harder. Every market-standard update had to be checked against a growing pile of custom rules.
  • Client onboarding went against the standard. Instead of accepting standard messages, we sometimes had to ask new clients to send our custom format.
  • Everyone felt the pain. Developers maintained fragile logic, downstream teams depended on DFP for every change, and clients faced extra friction.

What I did

  • Audited our mapping sheets to find every field where DFP applied a custom rule.
  • Validated each rule with the PIM team to understand why it existed and what would break without it.
  • Checked each rule against the ISO standards to see where we had drifted from market practice.
  • Ran a feasibility analysis on whether Prowide could implement these rules in its converter API without data loss.

The key decision

PIM's first preference was for Prowide to implement all the custom rules. The analysis showed this would only move the problem from DFP to Prowide: we would still be non-standard, just somewhere else.

Option on the tableMove the custom rules into Prowide's converter. Feasible, but the shared layer stays non-standard.
What I proposedProwide translates strictly by the standard. PIM owns its unique rules internally and retires them over time.

I walked PIM through the impact of both options. Once it was clear that the original approach wouldn't achieve the goal of the change, they agreed to the standards-first design.

Delivery

  • The solution is in development.
  • I defined the testing strategy and validation metrics for the migration.
  • I built the test suites in Zephyr to confirm messages translate correctly and no data is lost.

Expected impact

  • Simpler, lower-risk yearly SWIFT releases, because the shared platform follows the standard.
  • New clients can onboard on standard formats, with no custom-format requests.
  • Each customisation sits with the team that needs it, which reduces dependencies on DFP.

What I learned

Solving the problem and moving the problem can look the same on a plan.

The first solution on the table was technically feasible and the stakeholder wanted it, but it didn't serve the reason we started the project. Going back to why we were making the change gave me the clarity, and the confidence, to recommend something different and bring the team with me.
